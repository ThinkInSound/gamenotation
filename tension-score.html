A graphical notation for tension in games

Tension Score

Two small games that write their own score as they are played. Progress is the melody, tension is the dynamics, and recurring patterns are the voices. Tension combines what you stand to lose, how close danger is, and how uncertain the outcome is, which the game estimates by playing out 24 possible futures. Watch the bot, or play yourself and rate your own tension to compare it with the machine’s.

Rules
  • Goal. Pieces fall into a well 9 cells wide and 18 tall. Fill a row completely to clear it. Every 8 cleared rows is a new level, and pieces fall faster each level.
  • Pieces. Five shapes: D Duo (2 cells), B Bar (3 in a line), H Hook (3, bent), Z Zed (4, zigzag) and C Cross (5, a plus). The next two pieces are shown on the right.
  • Moves. Move left and right, rotate (a piece steps sideways if a wall blocks the turn), drop faster, or hard drop straight to the outline below it. A piece locks shortly after it lands.
  • Rising rows. After every 10 pieces, a row with one gap rises from the bottom and pushes the stack up. The counter on the left shows how many pieces until the next one.
  • Danger zone. The top three rows are tinted. You top out if a new piece has no room to appear, or a rising row pushes blocks off the top. The well then empties and you go back to your last safe point (every third level, with checkpoints) or to level 1 (with permadeath).
  • Order of pieces. Structured deals pieces in shuffled sets of five, one of each shape, so you can learn what is coming. After two sets, a set occasionally breaks and one piece is swapped. Random draws every piece independently, so you may wait a long time for a shape.
  • Controls. Press Play yourself, then use ← → or A D to move, ↑ or W to rotate, ↓ or S to drop faster, and Space to hard drop. On touch, tap beside the well to move, tap its top half to rotate and its bottom half to hard drop. The bot uses exactly these moves.
  • Goal. Collect the five sparks to open the gate, then step into it. Each gate starts a new zone and clears every enemy.
  • Hearts. You have five. A hit costs one and knocks you back. The enemy that hit you disappears, and you are safe for 1 second while you blink. If you are hurt, a heal (+) may appear after a gate. It restores one heart.
  • Dash. A burst of about three times normal speed for a fifth of a second. Nothing can hit you mid-dash. It recharges in 1.5 seconds, shown by the ring around you. The bot dashes the same way when an enemy gets close.
  • Death. With checkpoints, every third gate is a safe point, and a death returns you to the last one. With permadeath, a death returns you to zone 1. Either way your hearts refill, and motifs you have already heard stay familiar.
  • Enemies. A dashed ring shows where one is about to appear. Red triangles chase you and turn with some momentum. Amber diamonds fly straight and bounce off walls. Enemies fade, then vanish after a few seconds (motif enemies after 6). The longer you stay in a zone, the faster enemies arrive.
  • Motifs. Enemies arrive in four patterns: A Pincer from two opposite edges, B Crossfire sweeping across in parallel lanes, C Stream in single file from one edge, and D Ring closing in around you. A new one appears every two zones. With Structured order they repeat in a phrase you can learn, which now and then breaks.
  • Controls. Press Play yourself, then use WASD or the arrow keys to move and Space to dash, or hold a finger where you want to go. The bot plays by exactly these rules.

How it is measured

Progress, the melody

P = zone + 0.8 × sparks / 5, and entering the gate adds the last 0.2. Zones are the staff lines, so a run reads as a rising line, with a drop at each death.

Progress, the melody

P = level + rows cleared / 8. Levels are the staff lines. Bar lines mark level-ups, and fainter lines mark each rising row, every 10 pieces: the game's built-in meter.

Stakes

Progress since the last safe point, plus 0.25 for the time a death always costs, scaled as 1 − e^(−S/2.5). With checkpoints it forms a sawtooth. With permadeath it keeps climbing for the whole run.

Threat

0.4 × missing hearts + 0.6 × pressure. Pressure adds up every enemy, weighted by e^(−distance/55), with half weight for enemies still spawning.

Threat

0.7 × (stack height / 18)^1.5 + 0.2 × holes / 10 + 0.1 × unevenness / 24, capped at 1. A hole is an empty cell with a block above it, and unevenness adds up the height differences between neighbouring columns.

Uncertainty

Twice a second, the game copies the well and plays out the next 8 pieces 24 times: the current piece, the two shown, then pieces from the same randomizer, placed the way the bot would with some randomness. A future fails if the stack reaches the danger zone. If p is the share of futures that stay clear, uncertainty is H(p).

Uncertainty

About three times a second, the game copies itself and plays 24 possible next-3-seconds, moving like the bot with some randomness. If p is the share of futures without a hit, uncertainty is the binary entropy H(p): 0 when every future agrees, 1 when they split evenly.

Tension

T = (stakes × threat × uncertainty)^(1/3). All three are needed, and the result stays on the same 0–1 scale as its parts. The marking follows T: pp under 0.15, then p, mp, mf, f, and ff above 0.75.

Suspension and resolution

A suspension starts when progress stalls for 2 seconds while threat climbs and tension is above 0.35. It resolves when progress moves on, breaks on a hit, or fades if tension drops. The tension cycle is the strongest repeating period in the tension curve, found by autocorrelation.

Pieces as motifs

Each piece is a note in the motifs lane. With Structured order, the last piece of each set of five is fully predictable, and a broken set is marked !. With Random order, nothing is. Pattern surprise uses the same learning model as the arena. The bot scores every possible placement on height, cleared rows, holes and unevenness (after Yiyuan Lee’s weights), then moves the piece one step at a time. Instead of motifs playing at once, Zedrix counts open wells: columns at least 3 lower than both neighbours, each one a plan the player is keeping open.

Motifs and pattern surprise

A new motif appears every two zones, and one heard three or more times may come back varied (A′). With Structured order, motifs follow a repeating phrase that grows as motifs arrive: AAAA, then AABA (song form), ABAC and ABACABAD (rondo). Each phrase ends with two motifs at once, like a cadence. Once a phrase has been heard twice, each slot has a 12% chance of a surprise swap, marked !. Random order picks motifs freely. Pattern surprise comes from a model that remembers the last three motifs and learns as you play. It scores −log₂ P(motif | recent motifs), and the trace fades over 2.5 seconds.

The weights are first guesses and have not been checked against how people feel. The simulated futures assume a player who moves like the bot, so when you play, uncertainty describes a typical player, not you. To see what stakes do, run the same game with checkpoints and then with permadeath: threat looks similar, but tension changes shape.
