Tension Score

A small arena game that measures itself while it is played. Each moment is scored like music: progress is the melody and tension is the dynamics, with tension = stakes × threat × uncertainty. Uncertainty comes from simulating 24 possible futures, which are drawn on the arena as faint trails.

Rules
  • Goal. Collect the five sparks to open the gate, then step into it. Each gate starts a new zone and clears every enemy.
  • Hearts. You have five. A hit costs one, knocks you back, removes the enemy that hit you, and leaves you safe for 1 second while you blink. After a gate, a heal (+) may appear if you are hurt; it restores one heart.
  • Dash. A burst of about three times normal speed for a fifth of a second. Nothing can hit you mid-dash. It recharges in 1.5 seconds, shown by the ring around you. The bot dashes the same way when an enemy gets close.
  • Death. With checkpoints, every third gate is a safe point, and a death returns you to the last one. With permadeath, a death returns you to zone 1. Either way your hearts refill, and motifs you have already heard stay familiar.
  • Enemies. A dashed ring shows where one is about to appear. Red triangles chase you and turn with some momentum. Amber diamonds fly straight and bounce off walls. Enemies fade, then vanish after a few seconds (motif enemies after 6). The longer you stay in a zone, the faster enemies arrive.
  • Motifs. Enemies arrive in four patterns: A Pincer from two opposite edges, B Crossfire sweeping across in parallel lanes, C Stream in single file from one edge, and D Ring closing in around you. A new one is introduced every two zones. With Structured composition they repeat in a phrase you can learn, and now and then the pattern breaks.
  • Controls. Press Play yourself, then use WASD or the arrow keys to move and Space to dash, or hold a finger where you want to go. The bot plays by exactly these rules.

How it is measured

Progress, the melody

P = zone + 0.8 × sparks / 5, and entering the gate adds the last 0.2. Zones are the staff lines, so a run reads as a rising line, with a drop at each death.

Stakes

Progress since the last safe point, plus 0.25 for the time a death always costs, scaled as 1 − e^(−S/2.5). With checkpoints it forms a sawtooth. With permadeath it keeps climbing for the whole run.

Threat

0.4 × missing hearts + 0.6 × pressure. Pressure adds up every enemy, weighted by e^(−distance/55), with half weight for enemies still spawning.

Uncertainty

About three times a second, the game copies its current state and plays it forward 24 times for 3 seconds, using the bot's movement with random variation. If p is the share of futures that avoid a hit, uncertainty is the binary entropy H(p). It is 0 when every future agrees and 1 when they split evenly.

Tension

T = (stakes × threat × uncertainty)^(1/3). It keeps the product's property that all three are needed, but stays on the same 0–1 scale as its parts. The marking follows T: pp under 0.15, then p, mp, mf, f, and ff above 0.75.

Suspension and resolution

A suspension starts when progress has stalled for 2 seconds while threat rose by 0.15 or more and tension is above 0.35. It resolves when progress moves on, breaks on a hit, or fades if tension drops away. The tension cycle is the strongest repeat period in the tension curve, found by autocorrelation.

Motifs and pattern surprise

Enemies arrive as four motifs: A Pincer, B Crossfire, C Stream and D Ring. A new motif is introduced every two zones, and a motif heard three or more times may return varied (A′). With Structured composition, motifs follow a repeating phrase form that grows as motifs are unlocked: AAAA, then AABA (song form), ABAC, and ABACABAD (rondo). Slots are evenly spaced, the last slot of each phrase layers two motifs like a cadence, and once a form has been heard twice, each slot has a 12% chance of being swapped for an unexpected motif, marked ! on the score. Random composition picks motifs freely. Pattern surprise comes from a model that remembers the last three motifs, learns as you play, and scores −log₂ P(motif | recent motifs), added to a trace that fades over 2.5 seconds.

These are first-guess weights, not validated ones. The simulated futures assume the player moves like the bot, so in play mode uncertainty measures how open the situation is for a typical player, not for you. Try the same session with checkpoints and then with permadeath: the threat curve stays similar, but stakes, and so tension, change shape.

  3. Lerdahl, F. (2001). Tonal Pitch Space. Oxford University Press.Tension as distance from home; progress as distance from the start.
  4. Browne, C. (2008). Automatic Generation and Evaluation of Recombination Games. PhD thesis, Queensland University of Technology.Measuring uncertainty from simulated play, as the simulated futures do.
  5. Meyer, L. B. (1956). Emotion and Meaning in Music. University of Chicago Press.Emotion from expectations being set up, confirmed or broken: the idea behind motifs and broken patterns.
  6. Huron, D. (2006). Sweet Anticipation: Music and the Psychology of Expectation. MIT Press.Expectation and surprise as measurable responses; the basis of pattern surprise.
  7. Cleary, J. G., & Witten, I. H. (1984). Data compression using adaptive coding and partial string matching. IEEE Transactions on Communications.The back-off over recent motifs in the expectation model.
  8. Krichevsky, R. E., & Trofimov, V. K. (1981). The performance of universal encoding. IEEE Transactions on Information Theory.The estimator at the base of the expectation model.
  9. Cowan, N. (2001). The magical number 4 in short-term memory. Behavioral and Brain Sciences.Why layering more than three or four motifs may stop adding tension.
  10. Koster, R. (2004). A Theory of Fun for Game Design. Paraglyph Press.Fun as learning patterns, the reason motifs are introduced one at a time.
  11. Lopes, P., Yannakakis, G. N., & Liapis, A. (2017). RankTrace: Relative and unbounded affect annotation. Proceedings of ACII 2017.Relative, continuous rating, as in the rating mode.
  12. Hayashida, K. (2012). Interview on the level design of Super Mario 3D Land, Gamasutra.Introduce, develop, twist, conclude: the shape behind establishing and varying motifs.
